ElemenTrick

English Rules · 3–4 players

These are the English rules for the printed rulebook. You can also try a browser prototype (4 players against three AI opponents; choose English on the start screen). The prototype is an early version, and its wording and terms may differ from this rulebook. Where they differ, this rulebook takes precedence.

Today, once again, the sorcerers are competing to show off their mastery of magic. Illusions that deceive? Power that destroys? No. This is a contest of something far more fundamental: how skillfully one can command the elements of earth, water, fire and wind. They draw the four elements from the clouds of mana drifting overhead and clash them together across the field of magic power at the center. But let us say it once more: this is not a contest of strength. Tap into the "laws" of the field as they shift from moment to moment, and gather as many crystals of mana as you judge you can truly obtain. Who among them has the sharpest judgment of all...?

Players: 3–4 / Play time: about 30 minutes (4 players) / Ages: 10+

Overview

ElemenTrick is a card game of the type known as a trick-taking game. As the name says, you play by winning "tricks."

A trick is one round of competition in which every player plays one card from their hand, and the player who plays the strongest card takes all the cards that were played. Playing the first card is called leading. The other players must play a card of the same element as the led card if they have one. You repeat this until your hands are empty.

In this game, the aim is not to win lots of tricks. Instead, you declare how many tricks you will win, and aim to hit that number exactly. On top of that, the "rules for winning" (which card is stronger) change during the game because of Spell Tiles. (The place where these trick-by-trick rules for winning are produced is called the Mana Field in this game.)

In each deal (one round, from dealing the cards until every trick has been played), each player does the following:

  1. Draft Spell Tiles (take them one at a time, in turn).
  2. Reserve one of those tiles for a specific trick as a "Preset Spell."
  3. Declare the number of tricks you will win.
  4. Play the tricks, using your remaining Spell Tiles as "Reactive Spells."

* Details are explained in "Game Flow."

Hit your declaration exactly and you score big. The harder a number is to hit, the more points it is worth. You play as many deals as there are players, and the player with the highest total score wins.

Components

  • Play Cards: 60 cards … 4 elements (Earth, Aqua, Fire, Wind) × Rank (number) 1–15
  • Spell Tiles: 12 tiles … 8 Element Activation tiles (round) and 4 Reverse Rank tiles (square)
  • Trick Clock: 1 board, with Score Counter
  • Score Markers: 4 (four colors)
  • Score Sheets: 4 (double-sided: 4-player side and 3-player side)
  • Active Sheet: 1
  • Bid Markers: 13 (0–12)

Types and Effects of Spell Tiles

Element Activation Reverse Rank
Effect Activates the matching element. While active, it always beats the other elements. Sets the Rank to Reverse Rank. Smaller numbers become stronger.
Duration
PERSIST The effect lasts until another tile is placed in the same slot.
1T (1 trick) The effect lasts only for that one trick.

Differences by Number of Players

The procedure and the rules for judging are the same with 3 or 4 players. The only differences are the numbers in the table below. In the text, they are shown in the form [3-player: ○○].

Game Setup

  1. Decide who is P1 by any method you like. (After that, P1 passes clockwise each deal.)
  2. Place the Trick Clock in the middle of the table and set its hand to "1." Arrange the Score Counter around it, and place the Active Sheet beside the board.
  3. Each player chooses a color and takes the Score Sheet of that color. Place your Score Sheet in front of you, and set the Score Marker of the same color just before "1" on the Score Counter (outside the board).
  4. Lay out the Bid Markers with their numbers visible. (10–12 are not used in a 4-player game.)
  5. [3-player: Remove the 12 Play Cards of Rank 13–15 from the deck.]

* The layout shown is only an example. Set up in whatever layout is easiest for everyone to play.

Glossary

Term Meaning
Trick One round of competition in which every player plays one card and the winner takes the cards played.
Lead / Lead Player To play the first card of a trick / the player who does so.
Deal One round, from dealing the cards until every trick has been played.
P1 The Lead Player of Trick 1 of the deal. P2, P3 and P4 follow clockwise. [3-player: up to P3]
Declaration (Bid) Deciding in advance, and announcing, how many tricks you will win in the deal.
Rank The number on a Play Card (1–15). [3-player: 1–12]
Normal Rank / Reverse Rank The direction of card strength. Under Normal Rank, the higher the number, the stronger the card; under Reverse Rank, the lower the number, the stronger. It is normally Normal Rank.
Element Activation Cards of an activated element beat all cards of every other element. (In trick-taking games in general, this is called a "trump.")
Mana Field The way of winning that applies right now, as set by Spell Tiles. It is the combination of Rank (Normal or Reverse) and Element Activation (which element is activated, or none).
Slot A place where Spell Tiles are put. There are two kinds: the Rank slot (square, for Reverse Rank tiles) and the Element slot (circle, for Element Activation tiles). The Active Sheet and each number on the Trick Clock have one of each.
Preset Spell A Spell Tile reserved on the Trick Clock. It always takes effect in that trick.
Reactive Spell A Spell Tile kept in your hand. When the conditions are met, you can use it just before a trick.

Game Flow

The game is played over 4 deals [3-player: 3 deals]. Every player takes a turn as P1 once.

Each deal proceeds in this order:

① Deal Setup → ② Draft Spell Tiles → ③ Place Preset Spells → ④ Declare (Bid) → ⑤ Tricks, 15 times [3-player: 16 times] → ⑥ Scoring (end of the deal)

① Deal Setup (dealing the Play Cards, and preparing the Spell Tiles, Trick Clock and Active Sheet)

Shuffle the Play Cards well and deal 15 cards to each player [3-player: 16 cards each]. No cards are left over. Then lay out all 12 Spell Tiles face up in the middle of the table. Clear the Trick Clock and the Active Sheet of tiles, and set the hand to "1."

② Draft Spell Tiles

Do this after looking at your hand. Starting with P1 and going clockwise, each player takes one of the Spell Tiles laid out. Go around 3 times [3-player: 4 times], until all 12 tiles have been shared out. Place the tiles you take face up in front of you. Everyone can see who has which tiles.

③ Place Preset Spells

Starting with P1 and going clockwise, each player chooses 1 of the tiles they took and places it face up on one of the numbers on the Trick Clock. This is called a Preset Spell.

  • It always takes effect when the trick of the number it was placed on comes up.
  • Place Reverse Rank tiles on the Rank slot (square), and Element Activation tiles on the Element slot (circle). Each number has one slot of each kind, so up to 2 tiles can be placed on one number. You cannot place a tile on a slot that already has a tile.
  • Once placed, a Preset Spell cannot be moved.

Your remaining tiles become Reactive Spells. Keep them face up in front of you (visible to everyone).

④ Declare (Bid)

Starting with the player to P1's right and going counterclockwise (the reverse of the draft order), and finishing with P1, each player in turn declares how many tricks they will win in this deal.

  • You can declare 0–9 [3-player: 0–12].
  • You cannot choose a number that another player has already declared.
  • Take the Bid Marker for your declared number and place it on the same number in the BID row of your Score Sheet. Leave it visible until the end of the deal.

⑤ Tricks

Each trick proceeds in the following order.

About the Active Sheet

Preset Spell: Move the Spell Tile from the Trick Clock to this sheet, and it takes effect.

Reactive Spell: Move the Spell Tile from your hand to this sheet, and it takes effect.

In either case, if there is already a tile on this sheet, remove the previous tile (a PERSIST tile) and set it aside as Used before placing the new one.

1. Activate the Preset Spell

If there is a Preset Spell on the number the hand points to, the Lead Player of this trick moves it from the Trick Clock to the same kind of slot on the Active Sheet.

  • If that slot already holds a tile, remove the previous tile (a PERSIST tile) and set it aside as Used before placing the new one.

2. Place a Reactive Spell

Only the player who won the previous trick (= the Lead Player of this trick) may, before leading, place one Reactive Spell from their hand on the same kind of slot on the Active Sheet. You do not have to place one.

  • In Trick 1, no one can place a Reactive Spell (there is no winner of a previous trick).
  • Each player may place a Reactive Spell only once per deal [3-player: twice]. Once you reach the limit, you cannot place any more.
  • You cannot place a Reactive Spell on a slot that a Preset Spell was moved to in this trick.
  • A Reactive Spell that you never place goes unused when the deal ends.

3. Play

The Lead Player plays any card from their hand (the lead). The element of that card is the led element of this trick. Then, going clockwise, each other player plays one card from their hand.

  • If you have a card of the led element, you must play it (must follow).
  • If you do not, you may play any card.

4. Decide the winner of the trick

From the cards played, decide the winner in the following order.

  1. If a card of an activated element has been played: only those cards are eligible to win.
  2. Otherwise: only cards of the led element are eligible.
  3. Among the eligible cards, the player who played the highest Rank under Normal Rank, or the lowest Rank under Reverse Rank, wins.

The winner takes the cards played and places them face down in front of them. Place them so that the other players can count how many tricks you have won. Once placed, the cards cannot be looked back through. The winner becomes the Lead Player of the next trick.

5. End of the trick

The winner of the trick does the following two things:

  • Advance the hand of the Trick Clock by one.
  • Remove the 1T tiles on the Active Sheet and set them aside as Used. PERSIST tiles stay. A slot from which a 1T tile has been removed becomes empty: the Rank slot returns to Normal Rank, and the Element slot returns to no element activated (it does not go back to the effect of an earlier tile).

Repeat tricks in this way until the cards run out.

⑥ Scoring (End of the Deal)

When all the tricks are finished, work out each player's score following the "Scoring Procedure" below, and advance your Score Marker on the Score Counter by that many points.

Before moving on to the next deal, gather up all the Play Cards, Spell Tiles (including Used tiles, tiles left in hand, and tiles left on the Active Sheet or the Trick Clock) and Bid Markers (including those on the Score Sheets). Pass P1 to the next player clockwise, and start again from "① Deal Setup."

Scoring Procedure

Each player compares the number they declared with the number of tricks they actually won, and checks their score on their own Score Sheet. Look at the column of the number you declared (the position of the Bid Marker in the BID row). Advance your Score Marker on the Score Counter by the points you scored.

  • JUST: You hit your declaration exactly.
  • 1: You were off by 1 (the same whether you won more or fewer).
  • 2: You were off by 2.
  • OUT: You were off by 3 or more (0 points).

The closer a declaration is to the average number of tricks won, the fewer points it scores. The further from that average a declaration is (the harder it is to hit), the more points it scores, whether you hit it or miss.

End of the Game

After 4 deals [3-player: 3 deals], the player with the highest total score (the position of their Score Marker) wins. If there is a tie, compare the tied players in this order:

  1. The player whose gap in the final deal (the difference between declared and won) is smaller ranks higher.
  2. If that is also the same, the player whose declaration in the final deal is smaller ranks higher.

No two players declare the same number in the same deal, so step 2 always settles it.
